# ACTG Study Hub — Content Publication Policy

**Scope:** All contributions to this public repository. These rules aim to protect academic integrity, intellectual property, privacy and safe learning. They do not replace university policies or legal advice.

## Allowed materials

- **Original study notes:** independently written explanations, comparisons, diagrams and examples using facts and ideas without copying protected expression.
- **Self-authored practice questions:** new, general-learning exercises with independently developed solutions.
- **Original scripts and reproducible labs** used only in isolated or explicitly authorized systems.
- **Links to official/public references** and brief attributed citations consistent with applicable rights.
- **Synthetic and sanitized data**, with sample outputs clearly marked as illustrative unless genuinely captured in an authorized lab.

## Materials to keep out of the public repository

- Official lecture slides, PDFs, course handouts, recording transcripts or instructor-created diagrams unless there is **specific permission or a license** authorizing public redistribution.
- **Current or restricted graded assessments**, verbatim questions, answer keys, graded submissions, or reconstructed tests derived from nonpublic materials.
- Copyrighted standard texts (including paywalled ISO/IEC standards), chapters or figures without authorization.
- Student or instructor names/contact details, grades, IDs, emails, private communications, tokens, credentials, sensitive packet captures, or confidential system details.
- Instructions or evidence of experiments targeting systems without permission.
- Files taken from third parties simply because they can be downloaded online.

**Attribution is not always permission.** Crediting the instructor or linking to a work does not by itself authorize republishing protected material.

## Before publishing a change

1. **Rights check:** Did you write this independently? If it includes third-party elements, are redistribution rights documented?
2. **Academic check:** Is the content separate from any current assessment, restricted solution, or university platform material?
3. **Privacy check:** Remove real names, email addresses, student IDs, tokens, hostnames, sensitive screenshots and identifiable packet data. Review filenames and Git metadata too.
4. **Safety check:** Use local labs, synthetic addresses and harmless examples; clearly state prerequisites and scope of authorization.
5. **Accuracy check:** Distinguish observed facts from illustrations and hypotheses; prefer links to primary sources.
6. **Review:** Seek maintainer approval before merging. If permission or originality is uncertain, do not publish until resolved.

## Use of the MSc name

The program and course names/codes are used for **descriptive identification**, not to imply endorsement, collaboration, institutional approval or an official university repository. Do not use university logos or branded designs without permission.

## Licensing

The repository is public, but **no general open-content license has been selected**. Contributors retain applicable rights in their own works subject to GitHub's terms. A future license needs deliberate selection and suitable permissions from contributors and rights holders. Third-party material cannot be relicensed just by including it here.

## Removal / correction requests

Open a GitHub issue identifying the file and concern **without disclosing private material in the issue**. For sensitive personal data, request prompt private contact with the maintainers; do not post the data publicly. Maintainers should review and remove or amend problematic material promptly. Deleting a file in a later commit may not eliminate it from Git history, forks or caches; incident handling may require additional action.

## Disclaimer

Educational information only; not official course guidance, legal advice or permission to test systems. Always follow applicable laws and institutional academic-integrity rules.
