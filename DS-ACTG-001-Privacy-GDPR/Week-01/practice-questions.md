# Privacy & GDPR — Original Practice Questions

> Independent study exercises; **not** copied from any graded assessment. Answer first, then expand the explanations.

### 1. A university contracts a cloud provider to store attendance data. The provider only follows the university's instructions. What is the university's role?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Controller.** It determines the purpose and essential means of the processing. A service provider acting on instructions is generally a processor for that activity.

</details>

### 2. A mobile app needs an email address to provide its core service but also demands continuous location tracking for optional advertising. Which principle is most directly at issue?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Data minimisation.** Collect only what is necessary for the specified purpose. Transparency, purpose limitation and valid consent may also matter.

</details>

### 3. A controller follows security rules but has no record of risk decisions or compliance measures. Which principle is difficult to demonstrate?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Accountability.** The controller must be able to demonstrate compliance, not simply claim that it complies.

</details>

### 4. Does an ordinary service contract by itself justify processing a patient's medical history?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**No.** Health data is an Article 9 special category. A valid Article 6 basis and an applicable Article 9 exception, plus safeguards, are generally needed.

</details>

### 5. A website requires users to agree to unrelated marketing as a condition for accessing a service. What should you assess?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Whether consent is freely given**, plus purpose limitation and the applicable processing basis. If optional marketing is tied to necessary service access, the user may lack a genuine choice.

</details>

### 6. A public body processes records to carry out a function established by law. Is legitimate interest always the right legal basis?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**No.** The GDPR excludes legitimate interests as the basis for processing by public authorities in the performance of their tasks. Public task/official authority or legal obligation may be relevant depending on the law.

</details>

### Quick revision checklist

- Define personal data and processing.
- Distinguish controller, processor and joint controllers.
- Name and apply Article 5 principles.
- Distinguish all six Article 6 lawful bases.
- Recognize Article 9 categories and exceptions.
- Explain valid consent with a real choice.
