# Week 02 — Independent Practice Questions

> These are **new, independently written** revision questions, not copies or reconstructions of the graded/non-graded course assessments. Try each question before opening the explanation.

### 1. A student claims the OECD Privacy Guidelines automatically created enforceable rights against every company in 1980. What is missing?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

The Guidelines were **non-binding (soft law)**. They influenced later national and international data-protection standards but did not themselves create a universally enforceable treaty regime.

</details>

### 2. Why was a common EU data-protection framework useful when national rules differed?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

The EU aimed to protect individuals while enabling cross-border flows of personal data under more harmonized safeguards. Divergent national rules can create regulatory gaps and barriers.

</details>

### 3. An EU directive and an EU regulation cover similar subject matter. Does the directive normally need national transposition?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

**Yes.** A directive normally requires national measures to achieve the prescribed result. A regulation is **directly applicable**; both can allow details governed by national law in particular circumstances.

</details>

### 4. An EU agency processes its employees' leave applications. Which personal-data framework is generally relevant?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

**EUDPR (Regulation (EU) 2018/1725)**, which governs covered processing by EU institutions and bodies, supervised by the **EDPS**.

</details>

### 5. A national customs administration processes staff payroll, then separately assists in a criminal investigation. Can the legal regime depend on purpose and authority?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

**Yes.** Routine payroll is generally within GDPR. Particular competent-authority processing for specified criminal-law purposes may be covered by LED, depending on the exact circumstances and national law. The actor's identity alone is not conclusive.

</details>

### 6. Who normally adopts an EU regulation under the ordinary legislative procedure after a Commission proposal?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

The **European Parliament and Council of the EU** jointly adopt it. The **European Council** sets broad political priorities and is not a co-legislator.

</details>

### 7. A data-protection regulator issues a fine, and a business contests its legality. Who can review the regulator's decision?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

The **competent national court** provides judicial review. It may make a **preliminary reference** to the CJEU for necessary EU-law interpretation or validity questions.

</details>

### 8. A smartphone manufacturer ships connected hardware. A hospital operates that hardware as part of essential services. Which law emphasizes product security, and which covers qualifying organizational cybersecurity?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

The **CRA** emphasizes covered product-security duties, especially for manufacturers. **NIS2** regulates cybersecurity governance and incident duties of qualifying entities in covered sectors. GDPR may apply separately to personal data.

</details>

### 9. A courier app collects staff GPS coordinates and wants to reuse them for unrelated advertising. Does an EU data-sharing right automatically authorize the reuse?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

**No.** Where GPS coordinates identify individuals, the GDPR's lawful-basis, purpose-limitation, transparency and minimization requirements must be separately examined. Rights arising under another data law do not automatically waive those safeguards.

</details>

### 10. A website accesses device storage to set a marketing identifier, but the identifier is not linked to a named person. Can ePrivacy still matter?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

**Yes.** Terminal-equipment storage/access rules may apply independently of whether the information counts as personal data, subject to the relevant conditions and exceptions.

</details>

### 11. An individual asks a national regulator for damages following a breach, but has not shown any harm. Is a damages award automatic?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

**No.** Under GDPR Article 82, compensation is pursued through the courts and requires damage caused by a GDPR infringement; supervisory authorities exercise different corrective and enforcement powers.

</details>

### 12. An official says an EU act is valid simply because it aims to stop serious crime. Which fundamental-rights test is still needed?

<details><summary>Explanation</summary>

Charter Article **52(1)** also requires a legally provided limitation, respect for the essence, a legitimate aim, and **necessity/proportionality**. A good purpose alone is insufficient.

</details>

### Independent revision checklist

- [ ] Can I map the historical instruments to their organizations and legal force?
- [ ] Can I distinguish Charter Articles 7/8 and TFEU Article 16?
- [ ] Can I explain Commission vs Parliament/Council vs CJEU?
- [ ] Can I tell DPA, EDPB and EDPS apart?
- [ ] Can I distinguish LED/EUDPR (particular alternative regimes) from NIS2/CRA/ePrivacy (additional or specific obligations)?
- [ ] Can I apply the four-question regulatory-overlap checklist without seeing a sample answer?
