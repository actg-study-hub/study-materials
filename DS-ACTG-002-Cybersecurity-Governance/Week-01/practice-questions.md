# Cybersecurity Governance — Original Practice Questions

> Independently authored, scenario-based questions for studying. **Not official graded assessment items.** Expand each item only after answering.

### 1. A student-records system is available, but an unauthorized user changes a grade. Which CIA objective is primarily affected?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Integrity.** The record's accuracy has been changed without authorization. If confidentiality and availability were unaffected, they are not the primary failure described.

</details>

### 2. An organization deploys MFA and then reviews whether it remains enforced across accounts. Which activity illustrates due diligence?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Periodic review of MFA enforcement.** Due care is implementing an appropriate safeguard; due diligence verifies its continuing effectiveness.

</details>

### 3. A sensor alerts staff when someone enters a restricted room. What is its main functional control category?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Detective.** It discovers an event and raises an alert; it does not necessarily prevent the entry.

</details>

### 4. The network team swaps a firewall without a change review; an essential business service stops working. What governance control was missing?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Change-management impact assessment and approval** (plus testing/rollback planning). The change may affect service availability and security rules.

</details>

### 5. A hospital server room installs battery UPS units, backup generators and environmental cooling. Which security objective is directly supported?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Availability.** These controls help critical systems remain usable during electrical or environmental disruption.

</details>

### 6. What differentiates an attack vector from malware? Use a phishing email that delivers ransomware as an example.

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**The phishing email is an entry mechanism (vector); ransomware is a malicious payload/type of malware.** Attack vectors and payloads should not be classified as the same thing.

</details>

### 7. A security team restores files from backups after an incident. Is that a detective or recovery control?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Recovery.** Restoring operations after damage is different from identifying the incident.

</details>

### 8. Why is a defense-in-depth architecture more robust than relying on a single firewall?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Independent layers reduce single-point failure.** Segmentation, authentication, logging, physical security, training and recovery measures address different failure modes.

</details>

### 9. A training coordinator sends a fake phishing email and gives a short risk-recognition debrief. Is this more clearly awareness or technical-skills training?

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Primarily awareness**, because the stated aim is to recognize deceptive messages and safer behavior. It could become skills training if the exercise explicitly taught a practiced reporting procedure.

</details>

### 10. An organization discovers a contractor account being abused and immediately disables it. Name the functional control.

<details><summary>Suggested answer and reasoning</summary>

**Corrective**, because the response contains and corrects an identified compromise.

</details>
